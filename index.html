Ruanita

Colombian ruanas for little Swiss explorers. Wrapped in Boyacá, made for the Alps.

1 · The idea

The ruana, but for kids, in a market that has nothing like it.

A ruana is an open-front wool wrap worn over the shoulders. The kids' version in your photo is a hooded ruana-poncho with a soft collar that goes over a stroller or car coat with no zips or arm fights. Swiss parents love practical, natural-fibre outdoor wear (think Wald-Kita, Spielplatz, Wanderung), so the pitch is strong:

  • Easy: on in 2 seconds, no wriggling into sleeves.
  • Layers well: works over a pullover in autumn/spring and over a jacket in winter.
  • Story: a real Colombian tradition from Boyacá (Sogamoso, Nobsa, Iza) with real artisan partners.
Safety design rules (important in Switzerland/EU): no drawstrings or cords at hood/neck (the one in your photo has one; EN 14682 is the standard to design against), securely sewn closures only, short or no fringe, open sides so car-seat harnesses go under the garment, never between child and seat. Get every style lab-tested before selling (see step 7).

2 · Brand

Name: Ruanita (“little ruana”). Easy to say in German, French, Italian and Spanish, and instantly hints at the product.

  • Tagline: “Wrapped in Boyacá. Made for the Alps.” / DE: “Eingepackt in Kolumbien. Gemacht für die Berge.”
  • Voice: warm, a bit playful, proud of the craft. Short Spanish words sprinkled in (abrigo, ¡vamos!, mi sol).
  • Colombian touch: colours from the sign in your photo, a sun-and-mountain logo, a “Hecho con manos de Boyacá” hangtag with the artisan's name, product names from Colombian landscapes.
  • Culture note: the Muisca-style motifs are heritage designs. Use them only with artisan partners' blessing and credit them. Otherwise stay with playful, original doodles (cars, football, chiva bus, toucans).
  • To check: “Ruanita” is my suggestion, I have not run a trademark search. Check Swissreg/IGE, EUIPO, and the .ch domain before printing anything.

3 · Product shots (flat-lay) and playful prints

Laid flat, a kids' ruana is basically a square with a hole for the head, so we can show the whole print from above.

Variant A · “Abierta” (open front)

  • Head hole at the top, slit down to the hem, 2–3 sewn-on snaps.
  • Works over a stroller seat or jacket, easy to open when it gets warm.

Variant B · “Cerrada” (closed, like yours at home)

  • Square blanket with the head hole in the middle and no cut, so the back is covered too.
  • Simpler to make (no snaps) and your son already loves it.
  • Check: the hole must be big enough to pull over a toddler's head easily (my rough guess is at least 18–20 cm across, soft edge, no elastic). Test with real kids before production.

Shared construction

  • Square body: about 70–80 cm for ages 1–3 and 85–90 cm for ages 3–6 (my rough guess, to be set with a pattern maker).
  • Soft sherpa/fleece collar around the hole, no cord.
  • Contrast edge binding that frames the print and hides raw edges.
  • All-over playful print with a repeating motif so any cut looks good.

5 · Alpaca roadmap

  1. Phase 1 (launch): wool/merino blend. Proves demand with lower cost and risk.
  2. Phase 2 (after ~6–12 months): limited “Edición Andina” in baby alpaca or alpaca-merino blend from a Peruvian knitter. Alpaca is warm, light, not itchy, and Swiss parents pay for it. Expect a clearly higher price.
  3. Phase 3: traceable alpaca (herder cooperative), and a Colombian-woven alpaca capsule if you find a workshop.

Note: Peru and Bolivia, not Colombia, are the alpaca heartland. A Colombian workshop can weave the cloth if the yarn is sourced from Peru. I did not find a Colombian alpaca supplier, so this is worth asking the Boyacá workshops.

7 · Step-by-step: from idea to first sale

  1. Validate (weeks 1–3). Show these mock-ups to ~20 parents (Kita group chats, Instagram, friends with Colombian roots). Ask: would you buy, at what price (try CHF 79–129), which colour? Collect emails on a simple waitlist page.
  2. Lock the basics (weeks 2–4). Choose name, check trademark and domain (.ch), decide legal form (Einzelfirma is the simplest start; GmbH later). Ask a Swiss tax advisor or your canton's office about VAT registration and customs import.
  3. Write a one-page spec per product (weeks 3–5). Photo of your reference, size chart, fabric idea, colours, “no cords” rule, target cost. A freelance pattern maker (Schnittmacherin) can turn this into a proper tech pack for a few hundred CHF.
  4. Shortlist 6–8 suppliers (weeks 4–6). Use the table above plus 2–3 Portuguese cut-and-sew factories. Email them using the template below. Compare answers on MOQ, sample cost, lead time, certifications, and communication speed.
  5. Order prototypes (weeks 6–12). Order 1 sample each from your top 2–3 suppliers. Typical sample cost is a few hundred CHF per style in total (my rough assumption; get real quotes). Ask for fabric swatches first. They are cheap and show the wool quality.
  6. Test with real kids (weeks 12–14). Stroller, playground, rain, a wash cycle. Is it itchy, heavy, does the collar flop, can the child get out of it? Take photos. Adjust and order the second round if needed.
  7. Safety and certification (weeks 12–16). Get the fabric tested for harmful substances (OEKO-TEX Standard 100 class I for babies is the common benchmark) and the design checked against EN 14682 (cords). Confirm the exact Swiss legal requirements with the Swiss authorities (SECO / Federal Food Safety and Veterinary Office or a testing lab such as SGS or Hohenstein). I could not confirm the Swiss rules from my search, so don't skip this.
  8. Costing and price (week 16). Landed cost = unit price + shipping + customs/VAT + testing split per piece + packaging. Swiss retail margins usually want ~2.5–3× landed cost for wholesale-and-retail, ~2× for direct sales. Check duties and origin rules (EFTA has trade agreements with Colombia and Peru. Verify the current status).
  9. Pre-order launch (weeks 16–20). Sell a first batch of 50–150 pieces by pre-order (Shopify or similar, TWINT and card). Pre-orders cut financial risk and tell you the winning colours.
  10. Produce and ship (weeks 20–32). Place the production order, do a pre-shipment check (photos or a local inspector), ship to Switzerland, deliver. Collect reviews and photos for the next season.
  11. Grow. Add the Boyacá capsule, markets (Weihnachtsmärkte, Kinder-Flohmärkte), concept stores, then the alpaca line.

Email template for suppliers

Subject: Sample and MOQ request – children's wool ruana (Swiss brand, Ruanita)

Hello [Name],

I am founding Ruanita, a Swiss children's brand based on the Colombian ruana
(open-front wool wrap/poncho), ages 0–6, for the Swiss market.

Could you tell me:
1. Do you make private-label children's garments of this type? (photo attached)
2. MOQ per style and per colour, and your price range at 100 / 300 pieces
3. Fabric options (wool, merino blend, alpaca blend) and OEKO-TEX / other certificates
4. Sample cost and lead time, and cost to ship samples to Switzerland
5. Can you work without drawstrings and meet EN 14682?
6. Production lead time and payment terms

I can send a tech pack and size chart. Thank you!

[Your name] · [Phone] · [Website]
Money tip: do not pay a full production order upfront to an unknown supplier. Start with paid samples, then a small first order, and use payment terms such as 30% deposit / 70% before shipping. Watch out for suppliers who refuse to send photos or videos of their factory.
